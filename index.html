About

I'm a fourth-year PhD student in Linguistics at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, working in the Lupyan Lab and the Infant Learning Lab.

I'm interested in how meanings are learned, carved up in semantic space, and changed through use. Much of my work focuses on contrast, both as a tool for learning and as an organizing principle. A dog is not a book, but for a learner, it matters more that a dog is not a cat. Does contrast between the most similar, and most confusable, referents aid learning? How do semantic systems draw contrasts between words so as to minimize overlap while maximizing coverage?

Before Madison, I completed an MSc in the Evolution of Language and Cognition at the University of Edinburgh with Kenny Smith, and a BA in Linguistics and Cognitive Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I worked with Gareth Roberts in the Cultural Evolution of Language Lab. I then spent a year as a Research Specialist on the 1kD Project at Princeton.
